## Assessment

Character stat weights is the strongest usable roster-wide option in this test. It completes all builds, has higher overall and first-20 GG, and handles scarce meta combinations more flexibly. This does not prove every character's legacy weights are ideal.

Both GG modes fail their strict search for 28 characters. Four retain complete equipped builds; 24 finish incomplete, including 15 with no assigned mods. Nine lose equipped slots relative to the initial inventory. The fallback preserves only equipped mods not already taken by earlier characters. Chewbacca has just three remaining mods. His required three Tenacity sets cannot be formed even from the full inventory at his minimum dots: there is no eligible Tenacity diamond or matching triangle. Other failures develop as scarce pieces are used earlier; Commander Luke's matching triangle count falls from one to zero.

Among the same 41 characters with successful strict GG builds, average GG is 23.63 for stat weights, 23.48 for GG, and 23.49 for character-weighted GG. Therefore empty slots are the dominant problem, but strict top choices also carry a quality cost. Each GG search maximizes its own character given the remaining pool and strict constraints; it does not maximize roster-wide GG or guarantee improvement over existing equipment.

The character adjustment changes 33 builds but produces no clear roster-wide improvement over plain GG. Its generated preferences rank Speed first for all 340 characters. Wampa's weights are Speed 1.00, Tenacity 0.60, Health 0.34; Chewbacca's are Speed 1.00, Tenacity 0.56. Equipped-stat frequency mostly captures Speed's general popularity. Because GG already strongly rewards Speed, multiplying by this signal adds another speed preference. This is evidence that the preference model needs refinement, not proof that Speed is wrong for every character.

## Suggested changes, not applied

1. Prevent incomplete recommendations when the inventory can support complete builds. Add a completion-aware fallback, and explicitly report which primary/set restriction was relaxed. If strict-only behavior is desired, reserve existing builds or backtrack before taking their mods; simply retaining remaining equipped mods is insufficient.
2. Search the top two observed set combinations and primary choices as alternatives when the first choice is unavailable or materially weaker. Keep a visible preference for the first choice rather than an absolute veto. Avoid discarding an excellent existing build for a substantially weaker strict match.
3. Learn character-specific preference relative to population-wide stat frequency, with primary eligibility corrections. Preserve flat/percentage grouping. Actual extra roll counts would be a stronger future collection signal than only frequency and average values. Keep the moderate multiplier bounds initially; increasing them would amplify the current bias.
4. Allow essential speed/stat floors in GG modes. They currently bypass target stats, so high GG cannot guarantee useful turn order, required Potency, or survivability.

Recommended next step: fix completion and restriction fallback first, rerun this same snapshot, then tune the character-preference estimator. Keep normal stat-weight optimization as the practical default meanwhile.
